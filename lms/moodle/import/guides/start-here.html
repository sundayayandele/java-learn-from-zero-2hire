How this course works on ITLearning

  1. New to programming? Start with week 0. Take the Entry check quiz in the Week 0: programming on-ramp section (15 questions, about 15 minutes). With 12 or more, skip the on-ramp and start at topic 1.1; otherwise work through the three on-ramp lessons first. The on-ramp doesn't count towards the exam or the certificate.
  2. Work through the modules in order, a week at a time. Each topic has a Lesson page (read it and type the code yourself, then watch the embedded videos) followed by a Check your understanding quiz. Each activity shows a date: that's when to finish it to keep to the 14-week plan, counted from the day you enrolled. Every Monday you get a message with that week's plan.
  3. Take every topic quiz and aim for 70% or more. The quizzes are there to help you learn and keep pace, not to grade you: attempts are unlimited and you see feedback straight away. A topic counts as complete when you've viewed the lesson and attempted its quiz. If you score under 70%, reread the explanations and try again; the exam draws on the same topics. Topics marked optional (1.13 Kotlin for Java developers, 1.14 Localization, 1.15 Java 22 to 25, 2.5 Modules, packaging and runtime images (JPMS, jars, jlink), 4.9 Spring Boot with Kotlin) don't count towards the exam or the certificate; do them if you have time or your target employer uses them.
  4. Do the practice tasks and module projects listed at the end of each lesson and module.
  5. Build your capstones from week 6: Capstone 1 plus two others, then defend them in a 20-minute capstone defence (demo, code questions and a live change). Each capstone page has the brief, the architecture diagrams and a step-by-step build guide. Submit each one in its Submission assignment; it is marked out of 20 with the rubric, and 14 is a pass.
  6. Keep up the coding practice track (about 3 hours a week): auto-graded Java and SQL interview problems. Submit your progress at the coding practice checkpoint. On a phone? The Moodle app works for lessons, topic quizzes, forums and messages, but it can't run the coding quizzes: open those in your phone's browser instead.
  7. Sit the end-of-phase assessments: the exam once you've attempted all 31 core topic quizzes (60 questions drawn at random, 2 hours, pass mark 70%, two attempts at least 48 hours apart) and the 90-minute live-coding assessment.
  8. Grading and booking: mentors grade projects, capstones and the checkpoint within 5 working days, with written feedback, and you can resubmit until you pass. Book your capstone defence and live-coding assessment yourself in Book your capstone defence and Book your live-coding assessment; new slots appear every week.
  9. Get hired: work through the Getting hired section and submit your checklist.

Stuck? Ask in the Questions and help forum in this section. Say which topic or capstone, what you tried, and paste the error message. Come to the weekly office hour (link in this section) with questions, and meet the rest of your cohort in the Cohort lounge forum.

Phase 1: Foundations (weeks 0-14)

From zero programming experience to building your first production-style Spring Boot 4 services: modern Java 21 (with optional Kotlin), concurrency and the JVM, relational and NoSQL databases, and Spring Boot fundamentals. Every example uses banking and payments scenarios, because those are the systems Lagos fintechs, banks and global consultancies hire Java engineers to build.

Status: Phases 1 and 2 are fully built. Phase 3 is mapped in the full curriculum.

How to study this phase

New to programming? Start with the week-0 on-ramp: first programs, loops, arrays, strings and methods, at a gentler pace. Take its 15-minute entry check first: if you score 12 out of 15 or more, skip the on-ramp and start at topic 1.1.

Each topic page has three parts, and you should do them in this order:

  1. Read the lesson and type out the code yourself. Copy-paste teaches nothing.
  2. Watch the curated videos (all checked: real, embeddable, English with captions). They reinforce the reading from a second voice; they don't replace it.
  3. Check your understanding with the scenario CBT at the end of the topic. On Moodle it is the topic quiz: unlimited attempts, and aim for 70% or more. On GitHub the answers are folded under each question.

Then do the topic's practice tasks and, each module, at least one module project. From week 6, start your capstones. From week 3, spend about 3 hours a week on the coding-interview practice track: auto-graded Java and SQL problems in the format of the online assessments most employers use as their first filter.

Optional topics. Five topics are marked optional: 1.13 Kotlin, 1.14 Localization, 1.15 Java 22 to 25, 2.5 Modules and jlink, and 4.9 Spring Boot with Kotlin. Kotlin, localization and jlink matter for some employers and for the Oracle OCP exam but rarely come up in junior Java backend interviews; 1.15 prepares you for teams already on Java 25. None of them is in the exam or the certificate requirements; do them if you have time or your target employer uses them.

How much time this phase takes

Estimated effort for a learner who finished the on-ramp, or who already knows another language:

Work Hours
31 core topics (reading, videos, CBT, practice tasks) 75–85
4 module projects 30–40
3 capstones (Capstone 1 plus two others) 75–90
Practice track (DSA and SQL, 3 h a week from week 3) 35
Exam, capstone defence, live coding and getting hired 10–15
Total about 225–265
Week-0 on-ramp, if you need it 12–15 more

That is 16–19 hours a week over the 14 scheduled weeks. If you can give 10–12 hours a week, plan for 20–24 weeks instead and tell your mentor; the order of the work stays the same, and nothing in the certificate depends on finishing in 14 weeks.

Capstones

Ten financial-institution capstones built on public datasets that are free for commercial and non-commercial use, each with a recorded licence. Complete at least three by the end of the phase: Capstone 1 (the ledger) plus any two others. Capstones 1 and 2 have complete reference solutions, which your mentor shares once your submission has passed.

Deployment and CI/CD

Every capstone ships with the same pipeline on six platforms: GitHub Actions (primary), GitLab CI (secondary, free gitlab.com), Azure DevOps, AWS CodeBuild/CodePipeline, Jenkins, and Kubernetes manifests with Argo CD for GitOps, plus infrastructure sizing and step-by-step deployment to a VPS (for example SSDNodes), AWS, Azure and GCP.

Oracle certification

Phase 1 Modules 1–2 cover every objective of the Oracle Certified Professional: Java SE 21 (1Z0-830) and Java SE 25 (1Z0-831) exams. See the objective-by-objective mapping and take the OCP after week 14.

End-of-phase assessments

  • Exam: a 2-hour, 60-question scenario-based CBT covering every Phase 1 topic, taken on Moodle in week 14 (questions drawn at random from a larger bank). Pass mark 70%.
  • Capstone defence: a 20-minute recorded viva on your capstones, including a live change (format and rubric).
  • Live-coding assessment: 90 minutes, one algorithm problem and one change to an unfamiliar Spring Boot service (format and rubric).
  • Coding practice checkpoint: at least 70% of the Phase 1 weeks' practice track problems passing.
